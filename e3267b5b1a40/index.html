01 · The ninety seconds

Written to be spoken, not read. The timing marks are where to breathe, not where to pause for effect.

◆ 0:00 — the problem

Every company now needs a website, a brand, a backend, documents, a campaign, and someone to keep all of it saying the same thing. That used to take a studio — six people and two quarters. Most founders cannot afford it, so they ship something incoherent and lose on presentation before anyone judges the product.

◆ 0:20 — what it is

mythOS is an operating system for a company's identity. It holds the memory, the brand rules, and the governance in one substrate. Nova is the intelligence that runs on it — and it does not just advise, it BUILDS: the site, the backend, the documents, the deal papers, the campaign.

◆ 0:45 — why it is different

Every AI tool writes something and hands it to you. This one holds the whole company in memory and then produces surfaces that already agree with each other, because they come out of the same substrate. There is a human in the loop by design — one operator directing and reviewing, not a team assembling.

◆ 1:05 — the proof

Sixteen live properties. One operator. Every one of them a real domain you can open right now — a healthcare network, a federal contractor, a consumer game, a business-credit platform, a publishing imprint. Days to live, not quarters.

◆ 1:20 — the ask

We are raising to turn a system that works for sixteen properties into one that works for sixteen hundred. The engine is built and running in production. What we are buying is reach.

SAY THE NUMBER, NOT THE ADJECTIVE. "Sixteen live properties, one operator" survives scrutiny. "Revolutionary AI platform" does not, and in a room full of AI pitches it actively marks you as one of them.

04 · E-commerce — the to-do list you asked for

Shirts, caps and bags first, exactly as you framed it. Sequenced so that nothing later forces a rebuild of something earlier — the order matters more than the speed.

◆ Before anything is designed: who takes the money

This is the one decision that cannot be reversed cheaply, and it is a governance question rather than a commerce one. Merchandise revenue must never land in the same vessel as 501(c)(3) film capital — that is already written into the four-ledger workbook, and it is the kind of thing a funder's counsel checks. Naming the merchant of record first means the Stripe account, the tax registration and the storefront all get built once.

The sequence

# Step Why it sits here
1 Name the selling entity and open its Stripe account Everything downstream inherits it. Doing this fourth means redoing one through three.
2 Lock the product set — tee, cap, tote Three products, one drop. Your own audience already asked for a jacket, a purse, a visor and a cap; those are drop two, once the first has taught us sizing and margin.
3 Print-on-demand, not inventory No upfront capital, no unsold boxes, no storage. Margin is lower per unit; the risk is zero, which is the correct trade before demand is measured.
4 Artwork from the existing brand system The palette, type and marks already exist in the site's substrate. Merch built from the same tokens cannot drift from the film's identity.
5 Storefront on the site you already have Not a separate Shopify world with its own look. Checkout lives where the audience already is, and the brand stays one thing.
6 Launch to the wearables segment first The site already tags every join with intent — wearables is one of nine. Those people asked for this. They are the first email, not a cold list.
7 Sales tax registration where required Boring, and the thing that bites a year later. Handled once, at setup.
8 Measure, then widen Drop one tells us units, sizes and margin. Drop two is designed against evidence rather than taste.

WHAT MAKES THIS FASTER THAN IT SOUNDS: steps 4, 5 and 6 are largely built. The brand substrate exists, the site exists, and the intent-tagged audience list is already being collected. The genuinely new work is the entity, the Stripe account and the print partner.

05 · The back end, and what it does and does not replace

You said you want to get rid of iContact but need to see what this system does first. Straight answer, split into what exists, what is built-but-not-live, and what this does not replace at all.

◆ Status you should hear before the demo, not after

The audience engine is BUILT and NOT YET LIVE. The capture endpoint returns 404 in production today — probed this morning, with a control route to confirm it is a missing deployment rather than a permissions refusal. Joins currently fall back to email so nothing is lost, but nobody should say this is running until it is. It is waiting on a deploy that sits with Gheric.

Against what iContact does for you now

Function Status here Honest read
List capture Built, awaiting deploy Every join is tagged with the door it came through — ripple, soundtrack, wearables, trip, pendant, ambassador, app-waitlist, fan-campaign, reset-member. iContact captures an email; this captures an email and a reason.
Segmentation Built, awaiting deploy Segments are created at the moment of interest instead of guessed later from click behaviour. This is the part that makes the merch launch land on people who already raised their hand.
Newsletter sending NOT built There is no sending engine here yet. Keep iContact running until one ships — cancelling first would leave you with a list and no way to write to it.
Email templates / campaigns NOT built The document and brand system can render them in-voice, but the scheduling and delivery layer does not exist.
CRM / relationship history Partly — different shape The system holds memory: decisions, context, history across sessions. That is not a sales CRM with pipelines and deal stages, and it should not be sold as one.
Accounting NOT replaced Keep your accountant and your ledger software. Nothing here touches books, and anything that claimed to would be a liability rather than a feature.
◆ The recommendation

Do not cancel iContact at this meeting. Deploy the capture engine, run both in parallel for one campaign, and compare what each list tells you. Move only after the new one has demonstrably held a real send. Switching a live audience on a promise is how lists get lost, and yours is the asset.
