02 · What this does instead

It does not replace your numbers. Replacing them would be the same error facing the other way — our guesses standing in for your judgment. It decomposes them: given your revenue and a stated price, how many paying people does each line require?

◆ The proof that it decomposes rather than substitutes

Rebuilt from drivers, the three streams sum back to your published gross within 0.03% across all six years — and that residue is rounding, nothing else. $155,000 rebuilds to $154,953. $1,800,000 rebuilds to $1,800,119. If this did not tie to the dollar, the decomposition would be wrong, not your forecast.

04 · The one question

All three streams are served by the same audience. So the binding constraint is not any one line — it is the largest audience any line implies.

◆ This is the question for you, and it is the only one that matters

The 2026 row is the one to look at. Your model earns $155,000 in four months, and at these conversion rates that requires roughly 46,000 reachable people already in hand by December — not built over six years, but there at the start. So: how large is the audience today? List, following, everyone who would open an email. If the answer is near 46,000, this forecast is defensible in a room and we should go raise. If it is materially smaller, the plan is not wrong — but audience acquisition is the actual first product, it costs money, and that cost is not in the operating expenses yet.

◆ Why an investor will get here in under a minute

They will divide your revenue by a plausible price and ask how many humans that is. Everyone does. The only question is whether you did it first. Doing it first is the difference between a forecast that survives diligence and one that is discovered during it.

05 · What this does not model

Declared so nothing here is mistaken for more than it is.

  • Churn as a retention curve. Subscribers are an annual average here, not a monthly cohort decay.
  • The cost of acquiring the implied audience. If it must be built, that spend is real and is not in the operating expenses.
  • Seasonality. A documentary release drives a spike no flat annual line captures.
  • Film, festival, licensing, curriculum and grant revenue. Excluded from the engine by your own model, and left excluded here.
  • Price elasticity. Every conversion is stated at a single price point.
