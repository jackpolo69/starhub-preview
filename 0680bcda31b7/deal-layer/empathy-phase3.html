section 01 · The Precondition
Why capitalization is last

A raise without a budget is a guess.

Phase III cannot begin until Phase II's fully-loaded production budget exists and Natalie has accepted it. The budget is the input to the raise. Reversing that order — deciding the raise first and shaping the budget to fit — is the single most common way a documentary runs out of money in post and a creator loses control of their own film.

◆ What the ~$1M was, and what it becomes

The ~$1M figure that has been in circulation is Natalie's capitalization hypothesis, and it has been treated as exactly that at every phase — never adopted, never revised, never quietly used as a target. Phase III replaces it with the number Phase II produced by costing the authorized schedule. If that number is larger, the raise is larger. If it is smaller, nobody raises money they do not need.

Inputs: the Authorized Cut Mandate, the Production Schedule, the Fully-Loaded Production Budget, and the Rights & Clearances Register from Phase II; the four-ledger workbook; and the entity, grant, and existing-obligation information required to structure capital honestly.

section 03 · What Must Be Cleared First
Named now, not discovered at closing

Two things stand upstream of any raise.

These are surfaced here deliberately. Both are load-bearing on a waterfall, neither is StarHub's to resolve unilaterally, and both get more expensive the later they are raised.

Blocker Why it blocks capitalization
Chain of title — the PBC ↔ JS Films ownership position is undocumented: no signed work-made-for-hire agreements or assignments; fiscal-sponsor rights unreviewed; private-benefit analysis outstanding. Capital cannot attach cleanly to rights whose ownership is not papered. Any competent investor's counsel asks for chain of title first, and a gap here stops a raise at diligence rather than at the term sheet.
An existing origination claim — a second fundraising pair holds a discussion-stage MOU: 5% on funds they introduce, six-month window from April 1 2026, travel earned at $85K raised. An accrued origination lane sits in the waterfall whether or not the JV names it. It must be placed on the settlement instrument explicitly, or it collides with every participation negotiated after it.

Neither item is an accusation or an obstacle to the venture — both are ordinary and both are fixable. They are named at Phase III's front door because the alternative is finding them at closing, with money and a schedule already committed.

section 04 · What Phase III Is Not
The non-promises, in writing

Protective of everybody.

Phase III does not include or promise: a guaranteed raise · a guaranteed valuation · investor introductions as an obligation · distribution · a return · the transfer of Natalie's underlying IP under any circumstance.

◆ This document is not an offer

Nothing here is an agreement, an offer to sell, or a solicitation of an investment in any security. Securities counsel structures and papers every capital conversation before it happens. StarHub's role at this phase is to make the film financeable and the settlement honest — not to place capital.

Sovereignty is unchanged at the last phase exactly as at the first: Natalie retains authorship, underlying IP, and creative sovereignty. Any SPV is purpose-bound. Josh's existing ownership position is respected and unchanged. Custody was never ownership at Phase I, and it does not quietly become ownership at Phase III.
