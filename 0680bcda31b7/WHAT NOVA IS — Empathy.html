01 · The locked answer

Say this. Do not improvise around it. The reason the current explanation fails is not that it is wrong on any given day — it is that it changes with whatever was built that week, and a listener reads a moving definition as a product that does not exist yet.

◆ The full answer — about forty seconds

mythOS is an operating system for a company's identity. It holds one source of truth — the brand, the decisions, the history, the numbers — and Nova is the intelligence that works on top of it. What that produces is three things: the public surfaces (site, storefront, campaign pages), the documents (term sheets, decks, one-pagers), and the operational spine (who signed up, what they wanted, what was decided). The difference from an AI tool is that a tool writes you something and hands it over; this keeps the company in memory, so everything it produces already agrees with everything else. The website, the deal paper and the pitch deck cannot drift apart, because they come out of the same substrate. And it is not autonomous. One operator directs it and reviews every line before anything ships. That is the design, not a limitation — it is why the output is usable.

◆ The short answer — one sentence

mythOS holds a company's identity in one place; Nova builds and operates the surfaces that identity needs; a human directs it.

THE SENTENCE THAT DOES THE MOST WORK IS THE LAST ONE. "It is not autonomous, a human directs it" is what separates this from every AI pitch in the room. Volunteer it early. A listener who has to discover that limit for themselves treats it as a concealment; a listener who is handed it treats it as rigour.

◆ Never say these

"Nova does it all." "It runs your business." "Fully automated." Each is a claim the next question dismantles, and dismantling one claim costs every other claim in the same breath. What is true is bigger than what is overstated: one operator producing what used to take a studio is a remarkable sentence, and it survives scrutiny.

Her framing, verbatim: "when its said Nova / mythos runs your business a potential client hears — customer facing: ecommerce shop / website / fan funding site / membership site. back end: operations." Answering inside those categories is what makes the answer land. Answering in our architecture is what made the last one confusing.

Customer-facing

What a client pictures Truth Detail
Website / content site LIVE Multi-page, brand-governed, mobile-verified across seven device widths before anything ships. Sixteen of these are live in production right now.
Fan-funding site PARTIAL The audience engine is built — every signup is tagged with which door it came through, nine of them. It is NOT deployed, and there is no payment processing in it yet. Today a join falls back to email.

Membership site NOT BUILT And upstream of the build: the member benefits are not defined yet. That is a decision before it is a feature.

What a client pictures Truth Detail

04 · The JV share — three treatments of the same 30%

Her workbook carries a line reading "StarHub service & administration — 20.0% — applied to gross revenue," booked as a cost of revenue. That closes a question our own model had left open: her EBITDA does already net a StarHub share, and the rate in it is 20%. The ratified rate is 30% — but 30% of WHAT is still open, and the answer moves real money in both directions.

Six-year engine gross of $6,455.0K

Treatment StarHub receives She receives Margin she shows
A — her model today: 20% of gross, as COGS $1,291.0K $2,947.4K 45.7%
B — 30% of gross, as COGS $1,936.5K $2,301.9K 35.7%
C — 30% equity in the venture $1,271.5K $2,966.9K 65.7%
◆ The counter-intuitive result, and it is the decision

30% of GROSS pays StarHub 52% MORE than 30% of the PROFIT — $1,936.5K against $1,271.5K — because gross is larger than profit. Equity simultaneously hands her $665.0K more and lets her show investors a 65.7% margin instead of 35.7%. On cash alone, revenue share favours StarHub and equity favours her.

◆ What that comparison leaves out

A revenue share ends. Equity does not: it carries ownership and terminal value at any exit, which a percentage of gross never sees. So the real trade is cash-now against asset-forever, and it is not a spreadsheet question — it is a question about whether StarHub is being paid for building the engine or is a partner in what the engine becomes. Gheric's ruling, with these numbers under it.

WHAT SHE MUST NOT DO BEFORE THAT RULING: take the current sheet to an investor. It states 20% while the agreed rate is 30%, and a diligence pass that finds the operator's own share understated in the operator's own model damages her, not us. Say it plainly and early — catching it for her is worth more than any slide.
