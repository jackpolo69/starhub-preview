section 04
Assumptions · what an investor will test

Every driver, named.

A P&L is only as good as the assumptions a sceptical reader can attack. These are stated so they can be argued with directly — and so you can change any of them and see what moves.

Cost drivers

  • Merch cost of goods 45% of merch revenue — print-on-demand and small-batch curated drops, no inventory risk
  • Event and retreat delivery 40% of experience revenue — venue, travel, production, talent
  • Distribution and sales fees 15% of licensing — standard sales-agent and platform economics
  • Platform and payment processing 6% of fan-funding and subscription revenue

Structural assumptions

  • Distribution revenue begins 2027 — the film completes Aug '26 and premieres Nov '26
  • Experiences and immersive exhibitions ramp behind audience, not ahead of it
  • Research and measurement continues as operating cost after the completion budget is spent
  • No preferred return, debt service, or investor distribution is modelled — EBITDA, not net income

What this model deliberately does NOT claim: no terminal value, no series order, no streamer acquisition, no grant income. Templeton, brand sponsorship, and the 501(c)(3) donation lane through From the Heart Productions are all real lanes and all sit ON TOP of these figures. A model that already assumed them would not survive diligence.

The terms

30% joint-venture share on gross revenue through the platforms StarHub builds and runs — fan-funding, merch, and subscription audio. That is StarHub’s standard joint-venture rate — the same 30/70 split every StarHub venture signs — and it covers the build, the operation, and the fulfilment labour, not just software. No separate build fee and no monthly retainer: we are paid when the engine earns, and not before.

What 30% is, in dollars

On the modelled figures: $31K in 2026, $118K in 2027, $218K in 2028. Film licensing, experiences, curriculum, screenings and exhibitions are outside this — we take nothing on revenue we do not operate. Everything else is yours.

Plainly: StarHub carries the build cost and is repaid only out of revenue that would not exist without the build. If the engine earns nothing, you owe nothing. Terms are reviewable annually and the rate steps down as volume scales — to be written into the agreement, not left to goodwill.
