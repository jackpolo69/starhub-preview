The missing operating system for the company you are building.

We didn't invent AI. We built the operating system a company runs on — one source of truth for its identity, and an intelligence that produces every surface that identity needs.

Confidential · Prepared under NDA

Every company now ships more surfaces than it can keep coherent.

A website. A storefront. Campaign pages. Decks. Term sheets. Onboarding. Each one says something about who the company is — and each is produced by a different person, in a different tool, at a different time.

So they drift. The deck says one thing, the site says another, the contract says a third. Nobody decided that; it is simply what happens when identity lives in six tools and one founder's head.

Coherence is the first thing a company loses, and the last thing it can afford to.

Producing a coherent company used to require a studio: brand, web, copy, design, ops, finance. Six people, two quarters, and a retainer to change a phone number.

  • Most founders cannot buy that, so they ship something incoherent.
  • They lose on presentation before anyone judges the product.
  • The tools that promise to help produce fragments — a paragraph here, an image there — that agree with nothing.
  • And every fragment has to be checked by the one person whose time is scarcest.

The problem is not that founders lack tools. It is that no tool holds the company. Output without a source of truth multiplies the reconciliation work instead of removing it.

The cost is paid twice — once in cash, once in the raise.

  • In cash: a studio engagement to launch one coherent brand world runs into six figures and one to two quarters. Most early companies simply go without.
  • In the raise: an incoherent surface reads as an unserious company. That cost never appears on a P&L and is often the larger one.

What if a company could stay coherent while it moved fast?

What if launching a complete, coherent company — site, storefront, documents, operations — took days instead of quarters, and stayed consistent as it grew?

What has prevented it: every tool that produces output has no memory of the company producing it, so coherence has to be re-imposed by a human, every time, forever.

What that future looks like: the company is described once. Everything it needs is produced from that description, and everything produced already agrees.

mythOS — finally launch a whole company without a studio.

mythOS is the substrate: it holds a company's identity — brand, decisions, history, numbers — as one source of truth. Nova is the intelligence that works on top of it and produces the surfaces that identity requires.

A human directs it and reviews what ships. That is the design, not a limitation, and it is why the output is usable.

What no other tool in this category does.

  • It remembers the company. Not the conversation — the company. Decisions persist across sessions and surfaces.
  • Everything it produces already agrees. Site, deck and contract come out of one substrate, so they cannot drift apart.
  • It produces the whole stack. Public surfaces, legal and deal documents, financial models, operational spine — not fragments.
  • It refuses to invent. Governance gates sit in the path of output; unknowns are declared rather than filled.
  • It ships governed. Brand rules and layout validators run in code, not in a PDF nobody reads.
  • One operator runs it. The economics of a studio, without the studio.

Four steps, and the fourth is the one that compounds.

  • 1 · Describe the company once. Identity, brand, offers, constraints — captured as structured memory rather than a brief.

The math works at every stage — and it is built from underneath.

Every venture model produced on this system is driver-based: audience, conversion, price and retention build up to revenue, rather than revenue being asserted and costs subtracted from it.

Worked example — the EMPATHY commerce engine: a $6,455,000 six-year gross decomposes to roughly 4,167 paying supporters, 10,490 merch buyers and 5,093 subscribers by 2031, implying a reachable audience of about 350,000. The decomposition reconciles to the published gross within 0.03%.

That reconciliation is the point. The model does not replace a founder's numbers; it shows what those numbers require in human beings, which is the first question any investor asks and the one most decks cannot answer.
