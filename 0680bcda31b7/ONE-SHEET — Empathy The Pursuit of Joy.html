EMPATHY: The Pursuit of Joy

What settles a nun in meditation and a man in a maximum-security cell? From the Dalai Lama's monasteries in Dharamshala to a chaplaincy at Rikers Island — the first film that doesn't just document the medicine. It delivers it, and measures the dose.

What it is

A 90-minute feature + series led by bestselling author Dr. Natalie Petouhoff (Empathy in Action), director Josh Sikkema, and Grammy-nominated composer Maejor — with neuroscientists at Harvard, Columbia, Rutgers, and UC San Diego. Real people — monks, maximum-security prisoners, doctors, mothers — and a scientifically composed soundtrack recorded with the Dalai Lama's music school. Every piece is both medicine and instrument: built, measured, revised, tested.

Why it wins

The problem: the wise choice loses at the moment of need — the scroll, the drink, the cart are engineered; 3,000-year-old practice never got a delivery system.

The method: public translation — audience embedded in production from day one; empathy as R&D, science as the passport.

The proof: before/after measurement on real audiences, a peer-reviewed paper, co-regulation outcomes — what survives a skeptical room.

The field (and the difference)

Wellness documentary is a proven category — Heal, The Mindfulness Movement, My Octopus Teacher-class breakouts show the appetite. None of them measured their own effect, built for skeptics instead of the converted, or shipped with a distribution network already standing. This one does all three.
